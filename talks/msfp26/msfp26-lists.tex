\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Lists are important to functional programming, proof theory, homotopy theory.

They are defined inductively in functional programming languages, as cons-lists.

They are operated on by pattern matching, and it is an easy exercise to show that lists present a monoid structure, and have the universal property of being a free monoid.

List objects can be defined in the general setting of a monoidal category (Kelly, Joyal, Maietti, Saville and Fiore), and in fact, list objects present the free monoid, even in that general setting.

First, we give a string diagrammatic proof of this fact\dots

Second, we consider symmetric list objects in a symmetric monoidal category, meaning lists where adjacent elements can be swapped using the underlying symmetry of the category.

We show that symmetric list objects present the free commutative monoid, and we give a string diagrammatic proof of this fact as well.

This construction of free commutative monoids is a generalisation of the construction of free commutative monoids as higher inductive types in homotopy type theory (Choudhury and Fiore).

\begin{theorem}[Lemma~3.5 of Fiore-Saville]
  If $\cat{C}$ admits parametrised list objects for every $A$,
  then $\mathsf{Mon}(\cat{C})$ is monadic over $\cat{C}$.
\end{theorem}
