\begin{abstract}
  Cons-lists are fundamental to functional programming, and are well-understood as initial algebras, and as a concrete construction of free monoids. In category theory, such list objects can be axiomatised internal to any monoidal category, with nil and cons maps, and a recursion mechanism. When strengthened to support parametrised recursion, they can be shown to give the free monoid construction. We study a small variant on parametrised list objects called parametrised symmetric list objects, in a symmetric monoidal category, where the two frontmost elements are allowed to be permuted. We show that this gives a construction of free commutative monoids, and develop some related theory.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Lists are a fundamental data structure, important in computer science and mathematics. They are often defined in functional programming languages as cons-lists, generated inductively from a pair of constructors $\nil:L(A)$ and $\cons:A\to L(A)\to L(A)$, and whose eliminators allow for definitions by recursion and proofs by induction.

In category theory, these lists are understood as initial algebras $\mu X.1+A\times X$~\cite{DBLP:phd/ethos/Hagino87,adamekInitialAlgebrasTerminal2025}. This list structure can be axiomatised more generally in the setting of a monoidal category, replacing the cartesian product with a tensor, and removing the need for a coproduct by axiomatising the $\nil$ and $\cons$ maps directly with an iteration mechanism. These list objects generalise the notion of Natural Numbers Objects~\cite{lawvereFUNCTORIALSEMANTICSALGEBRAIC1963} and were introduced in Joyal's arithmetic universes. An internal language for them was studied by Maietti~\cite{maiettiJoyalsArithmeticUniverse2010}, and other variants were explored in~\cite{cockettListarithmeticDistributiveCategories1990} and~\cite{jayFiniteObjectsLocos1997}.

In all of these settings, additional axioms on the underlying category are required to reconstruct traditional list operations, such as list append ($\App$), from these list objects. However, in the minimal setting of a monoidal category, this notion of a list object can be strengthened to that of a \textit{parametrised} list object which supports parametrised recursion. It is shown in~\cite{fioreListObjectsAlgebraic2017} that this yields a construction of free monoids (see also~\cite{kellyUnifiedTreatmentTransfinite1980}) in monoidal categories. We give a string diagrammatic proof of this result, showing that if a monoidal category $\cat{C}$ has all parametrised list objects, then the forgetful functor $\mathrm{Mon}(\cat{C})\to\cat{C}$ is monadic.

Finite multisets are another important data structure that are the commutative counterpart to lists; and in fact can be axiomatised with only a simple modification to these list objects. In this talk, we will study the notion of a \textit{symmetric} list object in a symmetric monoidal category. We will identify lists that differ by a transposition of the two frontmost elements using the symmetry of the underlying category. Using this, we show the analogous commutative results that symmetric list objects are free commutative monoids, and that having all symmetric list objects yields a similar monadic adjunction between $\mathrm{CMon}(\cat{C})$ and $\cat{C}$.

In type theory, finite multisets have been considered as setoids over lists, where the equivalence relation is bag equivalence~\cite{danielssonBagEquivalenceProofRelevant2012,altenkirchDefinableQuotientsType}, or as higher inductive types of lists with an additional swap constructor to permute the two head elements~\cite{choudhuryFreeCommutativeMonoids2023}. These constructions use function types, but our setting only requires a symmetric monoidal structure without closure.

\begin{theoremrep}[Lemma~3.4.3~\cite{fioreListObjectsAlgebraic2017}]
  If $(\cat{C},\times,1)$ is cartesian monoidal, then the induced monad is strong.
\end{theoremrep}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  Since the category is symmetric, we only need to define one of the strengths. A construction of the right strength $\tau_{A,B}:L(A)\times B\to L(A\times B)$ is as follows.

  We can equip $L(A\times B)$ with a doubly-parametrised list algebra structure with the maps $n:=B\xrightarrow{!}1\xrightarrow{\nil}L(A\times B)$ and $c:=A\times L(A\times B)\times B\xrightarrow{\cong} A\times B\times L(A\times B)\xrightarrow{\cons} L(A\times B)$, and from the strong universal property, we obtain a map $\check{\iter}(n,c):L(A)\times B\rightarrow L(A\times B)$. This iterator is the right strength map.

  The required strength coherences then follow from the strong universal property.
\end{proof}

\begin{theoremrep}
  Let $(L(A),\nil,\cons)$ be a list object on an object $A$. Then, the following are equivalent:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item $(L(A),\nil,\cons)$ is a symmetric list object;
    \item $(L(A),\nil,\App)$ is a commutative monoid object;
    \item $(L(A),\nil,\App,\eta)$ is the free commutative monoid object on $A$.
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theoremrep}

\begin{theorem}
  If $\cat{C}$ admits parametrised symmetric list objects for every $A$,
  then $\mathsf{CMon}(\cat{C})$ is monadic over $\cat{C}$ via the evident forgetful functor.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theoremrep}
  If $(\cat{C},\times,1)$ is cartesian monoidal, then the induced monad $T:\cat{C}\to\cat{C}$ is strong, and further, commutative.
